📚 Import a whole binder at once

Scanned PDF or a batch of photos: each page becomes a recipe. Pages that continue a recipe are joined automatically, then you check one list and save them all.

Scanning tips: on iPhone, open Notes (or Files) → Scan Documents, scan every page into one document, then share it as a PDF. A scanner with a sheet feeder also works well. One recipe per page is ideal; flat, well-lit pages read best.

Spreadsheet (CSV): one row per recipe. Handy if recipes are already typed up somewhere.

Put ingredients and steps one per line inside their cell (Alt+Enter in Excel), or separate them with |. Leave meal type, protein or tags blank to fill them in automatically.

Good to know before you scan
  • One recipe per page works best. If a page has two recipes on it, they come in as one. Open it with Review, remove the second recipe, and add that one separately.
  • Printed and typed pages read well; handwriting doesn't. Handwritten cards will usually come in with mistakes and be marked ⚠ needs review, so plan to tidy those by hand.
  • Big binders take a while. Each scanned page takes a few seconds to read, so 100 pages can take 5–10 minutes. Keep this page open and on screen until it finishes: reading pauses if you switch to another app or tab (it carries on when you come back), and pages that aren't saved yet are lost if you close it.
  • Check anything marked ⚠ needs review. These recipes looked incomplete (fewer than 3 ingredients, no steps, or no title). They're still saved; find them later with the Needs review filter on the Recipes page.
  • The scanned pictures aren't kept. Only the text read from each page is saved, to stay within the free storage. Keep your binder or PDF if you want the originals. You can still fix a recipe later with Edit → Scanned text → Re-read.
  • Website links can't be imported in bulk. For recipes on websites, use the iPhone Share shortcut or the 📌 bookmark button, one recipe at a time.
